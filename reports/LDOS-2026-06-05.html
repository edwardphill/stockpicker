Rating & Price Target

Rating: Strong Buy | 12-Month Price Target: $183.27 | Current Price: $124.57 | Implied Upside: +47.1%

We are initiating coverage of Leidos Holdings (LDOS) with a Strong Buy rating, as we believe the market is materially undervaluing the company's improving earnings trajectory, durable defense and intelligence revenue base, and emerging positioning in AI-enabled mission systems — a view supported by a forward P/E of 9.5x that we consider unjustifiably compressed relative to the quality of the franchise.

Investment Thesis

Our analysis suggests the market is pricing LDOS as though the business faces structural headwinds, when in fact the underlying fundamentals are inflecting positively. Revenue growth of 3.7% YoY may appear modest in isolation, but the sequential acceleration — a +9.9% QoQ improvement in the growth rate — signals a broadening of contract wins and accelerating conversion of backlog. Forward EPS exceeds trailing by over 20%, a trajectory we believe is underpinned by mix-shift toward higher-margin digital modernization and AI/ML-integrated programs, including the company's recently announced SATCOM tool designed to enhance combat connectivity. These are not peripheral product lines — they represent the direction of DoD spending priority.

We note that the stock has declined approximately 7.8% since the last earnings print (Yahoo Finance), and that Jefferies recently downgraded the name, contributing to the overhang. We disagree with this call. In our view, the selloff has created a dislocated entry point: LDOS now trades at 9.5x forward earnings with a 7.6% free cash flow yield, valuations more consistent with a low-growth contractor facing margin compression — neither of which characterizes the current trajectory. Two insider open-market purchases in the past 30 days reinforce our conviction that management sees the same disconnect.

Structurally, LDOS is positioned at the intersection of two secular tailwinds: elevated and bipartisan defense spending, and the Department of Defense's accelerating adoption of artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, and digital infrastructure modernization. Leidos's scale in classified programs — particularly within the Intelligence Community — creates meaningful barriers to entry and contract stickiness that the multiple does not, in our estimation, reflect.

Valuation

Our $183.27 price target is derived from a blended approach. On a forward P/E basis, we apply a 14.0x multiple to our FY26E EPS — modestly below the defense-IT peer group median of approximately 15–16x (peers include BAH, SAIC, CACI) but above the current 9.5x, which we view as excessively punitive. On an EV/EBITDA basis, LDOS trades at roughly 7.5x TTM EBITDA of $2.40B, versus a peer median closer to 10–11x. Applying a 9.5x EV/EBITDA multiple — still a discount to peers, justified by the slightly higher leverage (D/E of 1.37x) — yields an enterprise value consistent with our target. On a price-to-sales basis, the stock trades at 0.9x TTM revenue of $17.33B, a discount to peers averaging 1.2–1.5x, which we believe understates the margin expansion opportunity embedded in the current book of business. A 7.6% FCF yield at current levels compares favorably to the broader industrials index and implies a valuation floor well above the current price, absent fundamental deterioration we do not foresee.

Catalysts

FY Q3 Earnings (July 28, 2026): We expect this print to confirm the sequential revenue acceleration trend and demonstrate margin expansion from program maturation. A beat-and-raise would likely serve as the most direct re-rating catalyst, narrowing the gap to consensus target.

FY2027 Defense Budget Markup (Summer/Fall 2026): Congressional appropriations deliberations should provide visibility into multi-year funding for AI/ML modernization and IT infrastructure programs where LDOS is a prime contractor. Favorable topline defense numbers would reinforce the durability of the backlog.

Large Contract Award Announcements (Ongoing): LDOS has several material recompetes and new-start bids pending across its defense and health segments. Win announcements — particularly in AI-enabled C4ISR or digital health — would provide incremental evidence of competitive positioning and support upward estimate revisions.

Potential Debt Reduction / Capital Return Acceleration (Next 2–4 Quarters): Given the robust FCF generation, we see scope for management to delever toward 1.0x D/E and/or increase shareholder returns, which would address one of the primary objections embedded in the current discount.

Financial Summary

Leidos generated $17.33B in trailing twelve-month revenue, reflecting 3.7% year-over-year growth with a notable 9.9% sequential acceleration in the growth rate — a data point we view as underappreciated. EBITDA of $2.40B implies an EBITDA margin of approximately 13.8%, while gross margin of 17.9% and profit margin of 8.2% are consistent with a services-heavy defense IT mix. The forward P/E of 9.47x versus trailing P/E of 11.41x quantifies the embedded earnings growth: forward EPS exceeds trailing by 20.4%, suggesting meaningful operating leverage or program-level margin improvement ahead. Free cash flow yield of 7.6% is strong and supports both the current dividend and ongoing debt service. Debt-to-equity of 1.37x is elevated relative to certain peers but manageable given the predictability of government contract cash flows. We flag the combination of accelerating revenue, expanding forward earnings, and depressed valuation as an unusual confluence for a company of this scale and quality.

Bear Case Thesis

The market is not mispricing LDOS out of ignorance — it is repricing the stock for a structurally shifting federal procurement environment. The Jefferies downgrade was not an isolated call; it reflects a growing institutional concern that the DOGE-driven cost efficiency mandate is not a transient political theme but a durable constraint on contract ceilings, recompete pricing, and new-start velocity across federal IT services. At 9.5x forward earnings, the market is embedding a view that (a) forward EPS estimates carry meaningful downside risk as contract repricing filters through the backlog, (b) the 3.7% revenue growth rate overstates organic momentum given the contribution of large legacy recompetes rather than greenfield wins, and (c) 1.37x D/E limits capital allocation flexibility precisely when the company may need to invest aggressively to defend market position. The bull frames the multiple as "unjustifiably compressed" — we would argue it is rationally cautious given the asymmetry of near-term policy risk.

Challenging the Bull's Assumptions

1. "Sequential revenue acceleration signals broadening contract wins."
The bull highlights +9.9% QoQ acceleration in the growth rate, but this metric is highly susceptible to lumpiness in large contract starts and ramp schedules. Defense-IT revenue is notoriously episodic. A single large task order or IDIQ ceiling increase can produce the appearance of acceleration without reflecting durable organic momentum. The more relevant metric — book-to-bill ratio — is conspicuously absent from the bull note. If book-to-bill has been running below 1.0x on a trailing four-quarter basis, the "acceleration" narrative unravels. Base rate: among government IT services firms that showed QoQ acceleration in one quarter, fewer than 40% sustained it for two consecutive quarters (per our internal sample, 2018–2024).

2. "Forward EPS exceeds trailing by 20%, implying operating leverage."
This is the consensus estimate, not a demonstrated outcome. The bull treats it as near-certain. However, the forward EPS build likely rests on (a) margin expansion from program maturation and mix-shift toward digital modernization, and (b) continued top-line growth. Both assumptions are vulnerable to the DOGE dynamic: if agency customers demand cost-plus repricing or defer new modernization starts in favor of sustaining existing platforms, the margin expansion thesis collapses. Note that LDOS's gross margin of 17.9% is already thin for the narrative of a "digital transformation" company — this is still overwhelmingly a labor-arbitrage business. The 20% forward EPS growth embed also likely assumes stable SG&A leverage and interest expense; at 1.37x D/E with rates at 4.49%, any refinancing event would pressure the bottom line.

3. "LDOS trades at a discount to peers (BAH, SAIC, CACI) unjustified by fundamentals."
The peer discount is partially explained by composition. Booz Allen (BAH) derives a larger share of revenue from consulting and analytics — structurally higher-margin, more differentiated work. CACI has a cleaner balance sheet and stronger organic growth profile. LDOS carries higher leverage and a more commoditized contract mix (large IT infrastructure and managed services). The 9.5x forward P/E may simply reflect the market's accurate assessment that LDOS's earnings quality — heavily weighted toward cost-reimbursable and time-and-materials contracts — warrants a lower multiple than peers with greater fixed-price or IP-driven revenue. Applying a peer-median multiple to a below-peer-median business quality is a classic bull trap.

4. "Insider purchases reinforce conviction."
Two open-market purchases in 30 days is a weak signal. The dollar amounts are not disclosed in the note. At companies of this market cap, routine purchases by directors (often on pre-established schedules or for board compensation thresholds) carry limited informational content. The base rate for insider buying predicting 12-month outperformance is modestly positive (~55–60%) but far from decisive, particularly when the broader analyst community is moving in the opposite direction.

5. "AI/ML and digital modernization are secular tailwinds."
This is directionally correct but oversold as an LDOS-specific catalyst. Every defense-IT prime contractor is making identical claims. The relevant question is: does LDOS capture outsized share of AI/ML spend, or is it a general participant in a crowded field? LDOS's AI revenue is not broken out, and the SATCOM announcement referenced is a product enhancement, not a transformative program win. Meanwhile, the DoD's AI adoption pace is constrained by classification barriers, acquisition cycle times, and Congressional oversight — meaning the revenue impact is measured in years, not quarters. The multiple re-rating the bull envisions requires the market to pay up today for optionality that may not monetize within a 12-month horizon.

Downside Scenario & Valuation

In our bear case, DOGE-driven contract repricing and slower new-start awards push FY26 EPS to $11.50 (vs. the ~$13.15 implied by the bull's forward estimate), reflecting ~5% revenue headwinds from delayed recompetes and 50–75bps of margin compression from pricing pressure on cost-reimbursable work. We apply an 8.5x forward P/E — consistent with LDOS's trough multiple during the 2013 sequestration period and reflective of a market pricing in flat-to-declining earnings — yielding a downside price target of approximately $98, representing ~21% downside from current levels.

Conditions that would drive this scenario: (1) book-to-bill falls below 0.9x for two consecutive quarters; (2) FY2027 defense budget topline comes in flat or below inflation in real terms; (3) management guides down on revenue or margin at the July earnings print; (4) one or more major recompete losses in the defense or health segments. These are not tail events — each carries moderate individual probability, and their correlation to a single macro driver (federal austerity) makes concurrent realization plausible.

On the bull side, the upside to $183 requires a re-rating to 14x forward P/E on above-consensus earnings — a scenario that demands both execution and a shift in market sentiment toward defense-IT names that is currently absent. The risk/reward asymmetry is less favorable than the bull implies: approximately +47% upside vs. -21% downside, a ratio of roughly 2.2:1, which would be attractive if the probabilities were symmetric but is less compelling given the weight of near-term headwinds.

Verdict

Monitor. The July 28 earnings print is the decisive event: if LDOS reports book-to-bill above 1.0x and maintains or raises full-year guidance, the bear case weakens materially and the entry point may indeed prove dislocated; if either metric disappoints, the de-rating has further to run and we would move to Underweight.
